How HotSync works

1. LoadRead 25 years of MODIS and 14 years of VIIRS fire data from NASA FIRMS
2. CleanKeep vegetation fires, remove low confidence and "always hot" places (factories, gas flares)
3. Make fairLearn the VIIRS ÷ MODIS "exchange rate" for each month (2012–2025), convert the MODIS-only years
4. ShowMap, burning calendar, normal-vs-now, daily view and CSV downloads

Limits: what HotSync can't tell you

  • No morning VIIRS pass. MODIS flies over at about 10:30 (Terra) and 13:30 (Aqua), VIIRS only at about 13:30 (and at night). Fires that burn only in the morning are seen by MODIS but not by VIIRS. The exchange rate learns this on average, but not for every single day.
  • Clouds and smoke hide fires. No satellite sees a fire under thick clouds or smoke, so a cloudy month can look quiet.
  • Small areas are noisy. A drawn area with only a few fires per month gives jumpy numbers. A drawn area uses its country's exchange rates.
  • Hotspot counts are not burned area. One big fire can make many hotspots; many small fires can make few. Fire power (FRP) helps, but it is not hectares burned.
  • Recent months use near-real-time data, which is checked less than the final archive data.
  • Before July 2002 only one MODIS satellite (Terra) existed. Those months use a separate "Terra-only" exchange rate. This is the least certain part of the history.
  • Some places are not vegetation fires even after cleaning: lava from volcanoes (Iceland, 2014 eruption), gas flares that are left in oil countries (Libya), or hot ground that MODIS sees but VIIRS does not (Djibouti, exchange rate 0). Countries with very few fires use the world median rate.
  • Daily numbers are for the whole country, and the map squares are 0.1° (about 11 km), or 0.25° in very large countries (2° on the world view).
  • The fire dots on the map are NASA's raw detections, drawn by NASA GIBS: they are not cleaned (factories and gas flares show too) and can't be clicked. All counts, the calendar and the downloads use our cleaned data.

Data & Sources

Data

  • NASA FIRMS: MODIS Collection 6.1 and VIIRS S-NPP 375 m active fire hotspots (yearly country files, 2000–2025)
  • FIRMS Archive Download: raw fire dots for any area and dates
  • NASA GIBS: satellite pictures (MODIS Terra 7-2-1, VIIRS NOAA-20 true colour) and the daily fire-dot layers for the whole planet (MODIS and VIIRS Thermal Anomalies)
  • Natural Earth: country borders (1:50m), used when processing
  • Map tiles: © OpenStreetMap contributors

Tools

  • Website: Leaflet 1.9.4, Leaflet-Geoman 2.17.0, Turf.js 7.1.0, Chart.js 4.4.1, PapaParse 5.4.1, font Inter
  • Processing: Python with pandas, NumPy, PyArrow, Shapely, Matplotlib
  • Colour ramps: ColorBrewer (YlOrRd, RdBu), colour-blind friendly
  • Code written with help from AI (Claude Code). The idea and the method choices are the team's.

We acknowledge the use of data and/or imagery from NASA's Fire Information for Resource Management System (FIRMS) (https://earthdata.nasa.gov/firms), part of NASA's Land, Atmosphere Near real-time Capability for Earth observations (LANCE) (https://earthdata.nasa.gov/lance) and NASA's Earth Science Data and Information System (ESDIS).

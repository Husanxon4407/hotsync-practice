Why "fair count"?

Two NASA satellite cameras count fires from space. The newer one, VIIRS (since 2012), sees about 4 times more fires than the older MODIS (since 2000), so old and new years can't be compared directly. HotSync converts the old counts into a fair count, so every year from 2001 to today can be compared.

How HotSync works
1. LoadRead 25 years of MODIS and 14 years of VIIRS fire data from NASA FIRMS
2. CleanKeep vegetation fires, remove low confidence and "always hot" places (factories, gas flares)
3. Make fairLearn how many more fires VIIRS sees than MODIS in each month (the "exchange rate", 2012–2025), then convert the years when only MODIS existed
4. ShowMap, fire calendar, now vs normal, one-day view and downloads

Limits

What HotSync can't tell you.
  • No morning VIIRS pass. MODIS flies over at about 10:30 (Terra) and 13:30 (Aqua), VIIRS only at about 13:30 (and at night). Fires that burn only in the morning are seen by MODIS but not by VIIRS. The exchange rate learns this on average, but not for every single day.
  • Clouds and smoke hide fires. No satellite sees a fire under thick clouds or smoke, so a cloudy month can look quiet.
  • Small areas are noisy. A drawn area with only a few fires per month gives jumpy numbers. A drawn area uses its country's exchange rates (on the World view, every square uses its own country's rates).
  • Counting fires is not the same as measuring burned land. One big fire can show up as many fire dots, and many small fires as only a few. Fire strength (FRP) helps, but it is not hectares burned.
  • Recent months use near-real-time data, which is checked less than the final archive data.
  • Before July 2002 only one MODIS satellite (Terra) existed. Those months use a separate "Terra-only" exchange rate. This is the least certain part of the history.
  • Some places are not vegetation fires even after cleaning: lava from volcanoes (Iceland, 2014 eruption), gas flares that are left in oil countries (Libya), or hot ground that MODIS sees but VIIRS does not (Djibouti, exchange rate 0). Countries with very few fires use the world median rate.
  • Daily numbers are for the whole country (or the whole world), and the map squares are 0.1° (about 11 km), or 0.25° in very large countries (2° on the world view).
  • MODIS satellites are old and their orbits have drifted. Since about 2023 MODIS sees fewer fires than before. This does not change the fair count after 2012 (it is what VIIRS saw), but "MODIS saw" looks low in recent years.
  • Some 2026 days use the other VIIRS satellite (NOAA-20), when Suomi NPP sent no data or only part of a day (and after Suomi NPP stopped on 2 Nov 2026). Those days are adjusted to the Suomi NPP scale with a factor learned on days when both satellites flew. They are marked in the calendar and the day view.
  • The fire dots on the map are NASA's own detections, drawn by NASA GIBS: they are not cleaned (factories and gas flares show too) and can't be clicked. All counts, the calendar and the downloads use our cleaned data.

Data & Sources

NASA FIRMS, NASA GIBS, Natural Earth and the tools we used.

Data

  • NASA FIRMS: MODIS Collection 6.1 and VIIRS S-NPP 375 m active fire hotspots (yearly country files, 2000–2025)
  • FIRMS Archive Download: every single fire dot, for any area and dates
  • NASA GIBS: satellite pictures (MODIS Terra 7-2-1, VIIRS NOAA-20 true colour) and the daily fire-dot layers for the whole planet (MODIS and VIIRS Thermal Anomalies)
  • Natural Earth: country borders (1:50m), used when processing
  • Map background: NASA Blue Marble from NASA GIBS; street map and place names data © OpenStreetMap contributors

Tools

  • Website: Leaflet 1.9.4, Leaflet-Geoman 2.17.0, Chart.js 4.4.1, PapaParse 5.4.1, fonts Inter, Bricolage Grotesque, IBM Plex Mono

  • Processing: Python with pandas, NumPy, PyArrow, Shapely, Matplotlib
  • Colour ramps: ColorBrewer (YlOrRd, RdBu), colour-blind friendly
  • Code written with help from AI (Claude Code). The idea and the method choices are the team's.

How to read it. Two NASA satellite cameras count fires: MODIS (since 2000) and VIIRS (since 2012, sees about 4 times more). The fair count uses VIIRS from February 2012 and, before that, MODIS × the place's monthly "exchange rate" (VIIRS ÷ MODIS, learned on 2015–2025). Numbers are satellite hotspots (fire detections), not separate fires or burned area.
